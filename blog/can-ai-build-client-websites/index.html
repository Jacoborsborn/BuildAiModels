Can AI actually build client websites? Claude Code, tested

Published July 2026 9 min read

The honest answer is about four hours. That is roughly how long it takes an AI coding agent to go from an empty folder to a complete, mobile-first website for a real local business, working from nothing but plain-English direction and the business's actual details. Not a mock-up, not a template with the logo swapped: a hand-coded site with real copy, a working contact form and pages that load fast on a cheap phone. Five years ago that was a fortnight of skilled work and the whole reason web designers could charge. So the question people are really asking, "can AI build websites for clients", has quietly changed meaning. It is no longer asking whether the technology works. It works. It is asking what is left for the human to do, whether the output is genuinely good enough to charge for, and what the honest costs look like. This post answers all three from direct, repeated use, not a demo video.

Quick answer

Yes, AI can build websites for clients. In 2026 an agentic coding tool like Claude Code takes a small-business site from blank folder to client-ready demo in about 4 hours of plain-English direction, and the toolchain costs roughly £15 to £20 a month with free static hosting. What AI cannot do is find the client, gather their real photos and facts, apply taste, or earn trust. The build became the easy part; the business around it is the product.

What an AI coding agent actually produces

Pointed at a local business with a clear brief, Claude Code produces a complete hand-coded, mobile-first website in a single working session: layout, copy, contact form, map, review section and image handling, at a quality that would have taken a mid-level developer about a week in 2019.

It is worth being concrete about what "builds a website" means, because most people picture either a toy or a template. It is neither.

What comes out of the session

You describe the business in plain English: a plumber in Redruth, covers these towns, emergency callouts, here are his photos and his Google reviews. The agent writes the actual files: HTML, CSS, the lot. It structures the page for the trade (tap-to-call at the top, services, photos, reviews, service area), writes serviceable first-draft copy, and edits its own work when you ask for changes. The output is static files, which matters more than it sounds, because static files are fast, hard to hack and free to host.

What it looks like on first pass

Straight out of the agent the site is clean, complete and slightly generic. Think competent junior designer: everything present and working, nothing distinctive yet. The distance between that and something a client says yes to on the phone is the human pass covered below, and it is a couple of hours, not a couple of weeks.

Where "no code" is honestly true

You do not write or read code to do this. Direction happens in English, corrections happen in English ("make the phone number bigger", "the green is wrong for a roofer, go darker"). What you are really doing is art direction and quality control, which is why the people best at this are often not programmers at all.

The four hours, broken down

A realistic blank-folder-to-demo build splits into roughly half an hour of brief-writing, an hour and a half of the agent building, and two hours of human polish, which means the human still spends more time on the site than the AI does.

The headline "AI builds it in hours" hides a distribution worth knowing before you promise a client anything.

The brief decides the build

Ten minutes of specific facts (real towns, real services, real prices, the customer's actual reviews) produces a dramatically better site than "make a website for a plumber". The agent amplifies whatever you give it. Vague in, generic out.

Revisions are conversational

The middle stretch is a loop: look at the page, say what is wrong, look again. Because the agent edits its own files, a layout change that would once have been an awkward email to a developer is a sentence. This loop is where a beginner's taste improves fastest, because feedback is instant.

What still needs a human, and always will

AI removes the coding, but every part of the job that touches another person survives untouched: choosing the niche, finding the businesses, the phone call, the client's real photos and facts, taste, and the trust that makes a local business hand over £100 a month.

This is the section the "AI builds websites, get rich" videos skip, and it is where the actual business lives.

The truth-check nobody automates

An AI will happily write "over 20 years of experience" for a business that has nine. Every claim on a client site has to be verified by a person who can be embarrassed, because the client's name is on it and yours is behind it. This is unglamorous and non-negotiable.

Taste is the moat now

When everyone has access to the same agent, output quality converges and judgement separates. The decisions that make a site feel expensive (which photo leads, what gets cut, what the headline promises) are exactly the decisions the agent defers to you. That is good news: taste is learnable in weeks, coding took years.

The honest framing: AI did not automate web design. It automated the typing. The design decisions, the client's facts and the relationship are still yours, which is precisely why clients still pay a person rather than prompting an AI themselves.

The honest costs of the toolchain

The complete working stack for building and hosting client websites with AI is roughly £15 to £20 a month: a paid Claude plan at about £15 to £18 on Anthropic's published pricing, free static hosting on Cloudflare Pages, and about £10 a year per client domain.

Nothing here needs a business loan, which is most of the appeal against nearly every other 2026 side-hustle idea.

What you pay

The one real subscription is the AI itself: a paid Claude plan runs in the region of £15 to £18 a month, per Anthropic's pricing page. Static sites host free on Cloudflare Pages, which serves them globally without a hosting bill. A .co.uk domain is around £10 a year per client. There is no server to rent and nothing that scales in cost as you add clients.

Where this fits in a real business

An AI-built website only makes money inside a system: a niche worth serving, a lead list, a demo the prospect can click before they pay, and a monthly plan to sell, which is why the tool alone has made almost nobody an income.

The uncomfortable truth about "can AI build client websites" is that the yes is worthless on its own. Plenty of people have the tool; very few have clients.

The demo-first advantage

Because a build costs hours instead of weeks, you can afford to build the site before the sale: mock up a business's new site, then show it to them finished. That flips the hardest moment in selling (asking a stranger to imagine something) into the easiest (asking them to look at something). It is the single biggest commercial consequence of AI building, and most people miss it entirely.

Can AI build client websites: FAQ

Can AI build websites for clients in 2026?

Yes. An AI coding agent like Claude Code can take a small-business website from a blank folder to a client-ready demo in roughly four hours of plain-English direction, including a mobile-first layout, real copy and contact forms. What it cannot do is choose the niche, gather the client's photos and facts, exercise taste, or win the client's trust in the first place. The building is now the easy 20 percent; the business around it is the other 80.

Do you need to know how to code to build client sites with AI?

No. You direct the agent in plain English: describe the business, the pages, the tone, and paste in the client's real details. Reading a little HTML helps you spot problems faster, but it is a nice-to-have, not a barrier. The skills that actually decide whether the site wins work are editorial: knowing a good headline from a bad one, insisting on real photos over stock, and checking every claim on the page is true. Those take judgement, not a computer science degree.

What does it cost to build client websites with AI?

The full working toolchain is roughly £15 to £20 a month. A paid Claude plan is about £15 to £18 a month on Anthropic's published pricing, hosting for static client sites is free on platforms like Cloudflare Pages, and a .co.uk domain runs about £10 a year per client. Set that against one client on a £100 a month done-for-you plan and the entire toolchain is covered five times over by the first retainer.

Is an AI-built website good enough to charge money for?

Yes, provided a human owns the last mile. Straight out of the agent, a site is typically clean, fast and complete but slightly generic. The paid-for difference comes from the pass a person does afterwards: swapping stock images for the client's real job photos, tightening the headline, checking prices and areas are accurate, and reading every line aloud. That last 20 percent is what separates a demo from something a business proudly points customers at, and it is where your fee is earned.

Which AI tool is best for building client websites?

For a non-coder selling to local businesses, an agentic coding tool such as Claude Code is the strongest fit in 2026, because it works from plain-English direction, edits its own files, and produces hand-coded static sites that load fast and host free. DIY builders like Wix are easier on day one but lock you into their templates, their hosting fees and their look. The hand-coded route gives you something a template cannot: sites that are genuinely yours to sell, host and maintain.
